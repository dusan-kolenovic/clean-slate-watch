Clean Slate Watch

Can we build a single source of truth for state record-sealing laws that shrink the pool of criminal records we can sell? I spent today testing the actual sources. Here is what holds up and what does not.

Prepared for HireRight product & compliance Date 14 September 2026 Scope Source validation only — no system built yet
Verdict

Feasible. The hard part is not getting the data.

Every US state publishes its own bill activity, and at least one state — Virginia — publishes it as an hourly-refreshed CSV with no API key, no login and no scraping. I pulled all 3,646 bills from Virginia's 2026 session this afternoon and found the one that matters in about four seconds.

The hard part is filtering and trusting. My first keyword filter ranked a DMV data-sharing bill above everything else and missed the single enacted law that moves Virginia's sealing dates. And Virginia's own published status flags said zero bills passed in 2025, which is false. Both problems are solvable, but they are the actual engineering work — not the collection.

No existing product does this from HireRight's angle. The trackers that exist watch bills. We need to watch when records physically leave the pool, which is a different and later event.

And none of it needs an account. Every source on the critical path below is open — including a 10.2 GB public-domain database of all 50 states' legislation that I verified downloading today. Phase 2 can be built and run without signing up for, procuring or paying for anything.

Why SB230 matters

One bill, three dates, a measurable hole in the record pool

SB230 is exactly the signal we need and exactly the one a bill tracker would under-rate. It is not a new clean slate law. It is a law that moves the dates on an existing one — and the dates are when records stop being available to us.

Enacted 14 May 2026 SB230 signed, Chapter 1127. Amends the sealing schedule.
Live now 1 Jul 2026 First conviction records sealable in Virginia's history. Automatic sealing begins.
Next 1 Oct 2026 Automatic sealing widens: petit larceny, concealment, disorderly conduct, certain trespass, marijuana PWID.
Then 1 Dec 2026 Petition eligibility broadens; “otherwise dismissed” language repealed.

Staged dates per Virginia's 2026 enactments. Each stop is a step change in what a Virginia search can return — and 1 October is eleven weekends away.

Problems found

Three things that will break this if we ignore them

01

Keyword matching fails in both directions

My first filter returned three hits from 3,646 bills. Top-ranked was a Department of Motor Vehicles data-release bill. It missed SB230 — the only enacted law in the set — because the phrase was “court records” and my pattern expected “court record.”

The vocabulary is genuinely slippery: seal, expunge, set aside, vacatur, nondisclosure, record relief, plus “sealed bids” and “diploma seal” as decoys.

Fix: retrieve broadly with loose patterns, then have a model read each candidate and answer one question — does this change which records we can report, and from when? Roughly 4 candidates per state per session reach a model. That is cents, not dollars.
02

The official status flags are wrong

Virginia's own BILLS.CSV for the 2025 session flags zero of 3,510 bills as approved. Two of them — HB2723 and SB1466 — became Chapters 671 and 634 on 2 April 2025. Both are record-sealing laws. Both would have been silently dropped.

The separate history file has it right. A pipeline that trusts the convenient summary column would have reported “no activity” during the exact session Virginia reformed its expungement law.

Fix: derive status from the action history, never from a summary flag. Alert when a source's own two files disagree — that disagreement is itself a signal.
03

Passage is not the event we care about

A law passing costs us nothing on the day it passes. We lose records when a court system actually seals them — often years later, sometimes never. New York's law took effect in November 2024 with courts given until November 2027 to finish the plumbing. Delaware's rollout is publicly stalled. Michigan, four years in, has cleared roughly 1.6 million records.

Bill trackers stop at enactment. That is the wrong stopping point for a revenue model.

Fix: every tracked law carries a second lifecycle — enacted → effective → implemented → records observably absent. The last step is measurable against our own search returns.

Phase 2

What I would build, and how long

Three layers, one daily run, one place to look — built entirely on open sources. Estimate assumes the four states above and no integration into existing HireRight systems.

Layer 1 · Collect

Pull, don't scrape

Virginia's hourly CSV direct. Illinois, Michigan and Texas from the Open States bulk database. CCRC and Google News RSS alongside, covering anything breaking in the other 46. No keys, no accounts, no scraping. Every fetch records its own health, so a source going quiet raises a flag instead of passing for calm.

Layer 2 · Classify

A model reads the shortlist

Loose patterns cut thousands of bills to a handful; a model reads those and returns structured fields — scope, automatic or petition, effective dates, retroactive or not, and whether it obliges us to delete records we already hold. Anything uncertain goes to a human queue rather than being guessed.

Layer 3 · Notify

A digest and a board

One daily message with only what changed, graded by how soon it bites. Behind it, a standing board of every tracked law and where it sits in its lifecycle — the single source of truth. Silence on a quiet day, not a daily wall of noise.

Estimate: four days to a working prototype covering the four states, running daily, posting a digest. Add two days if Illinois and Michigan need per-state handling rather than an aggregator.

Open

Three decisions and one question

  • How fresh does it need to be? The open stack gives news within minutes and structured data monthly. Structured daily deltas across all 50 states is the one thing only an account-gated tracker sells. My read: we don't need it yet.
  • Where the alert lands. Slack channel, email digest, or a Confluence page. This decides the last layer and nothing else.
  • Who reads the uncertain queue. The model will hand back roughly one ambiguous item a week. Without a named owner it silently becomes a backlog.
  • PBSA login. We are already members. Their legislative centre is the one source written from our perspective, and I could not see it today.

All source tests run 14 September 2026 from a single machine; HTTP failures may reflect this network as much as the source, and every one is worth a second attempt from HireRight infrastructure before we write a source off. Bill outcomes above are from primary state records. State counts and effective dates outside Virginia come from secondary reporting and should be confirmed against the state's own record before anything commercial rests on them.
